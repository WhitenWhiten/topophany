03 Architecture

One binary, three roles

topo is the client, the collector and the observer. No CGO, no database, no external modules.

Two collectors, no consensus theatre

Read-only by design

  1. observer
  2. OpenSSH -Wfirst hop, host key verified

  5. SOCKS5remote DNS
  6. target

Up to 12 hops. HTTP, TCP, TLS and SSH checks run through every one, in order.

Read-only adapters

  • MihomoGET /proxiesmode, chain and selection, as the core reports them
  • Tailscaletailscale status --jsonpeer online, direct or relayed
  • WireGuardwg show wg0 dumplast handshake and byte counts; keys never kept
  • cloudflaredGET /readytunnel sessions and their colos, as the connector reports them

What a tool reports is shown as reported, never as an end-to-end pass.

Edges and platforms

  • CloudflareedgeWorkers and Pages; the POP that answered
  • VerceledgeFunctions; the edge and function region
  • Supabaseplatformmanaged services, in their region

An answer is credited to the edge that sent it, never to the origin behind it.

05 With your agent

Let your agent draw the map

Describing a network is the slow part of monitoring it, and the coding agent you already use is good at it. It reads what your machines already say about themselves, writes the topology, and checks it with topo validate before anything runs.

Ask it

Write a Topophany topology of my network to topology.json. Learn the format from `topo preview --print-topology` and docs/REFERENCE.md in github.com/WhitenWhiten/topophany. Read my SSH config, `tailscale status`, and the Compose files and reverse-proxy configs you can find. Declare each machine with where it is, the services on it, the applications they belong to and the addresses they answer on, add checks from the machines I will run workers on, and write what you learn about each machine into its notes. Never put a secret in the file. Run `topo validate --config topology.json` until it passes, then tell me what you found and what you guessed.

Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor or any agent that can run commands. Secrets stay out: the format names environment variables and key files, never what is in them.

Then keep it

  • A memo of your networktopology.jsonOne reviewed file says what runs where and how each machine is reached, with notes and runbooks beside them. Your agent reads it before touching anything; you review its edits as a diff.
  • A quick interfacetopo status · show · historyText or JSON in one command, for you or your agent: is anything wrong, what runs on that server, how a check fared overnight.
  • Before anything runstopo preview --configPreview draws your own network on synthetic evidence, so the two of you can look it over before a single worker starts.

06 On your Mac

Your network in the menu bar

topophany-bar is Topophany’s macOS companion. It reads your collectors through topo: the status sits in the menu bar, a panel shows where a problem is seen, and desktop widgets keep servers and services in view.

topophany-bar

macOS 26 or later · pre-release, built from source

topophany-bar on GitHub
  • At a glanceThe status as an icon, a count or a few words. In White Night, the built-in dithered sky, the moon is the status: full when all is clear, eclipsed while something is degraded, a blood moon while something is down.
  • Where it is seenIncidents, the applications they touch and the observers reporting them, in one panel. Open any of them in the terminal UI for the evidence.
  • Beszel and Komari, directlyPick servers from a Beszel Hub or a Komari server and watch their CPU, memory and disk, with no collector to run.
  • Widgets and themesStatus, server, service, map and time-zone widgets, in six themes, from native macOS to the terminal UI printed on paper.

07 Designed for iPhone Duo

The map above the hinge

There's no iPhone app yet. What there is, is the TUI's design for one: --device lays the TUI out for the screen of an iPhone, an iPad, or iPhone Duo in each of its poses, and simulates that screen in your terminal. On the open Duo, what you look at and what you touch go on either side of the crease.

  • Look up, touch downLaid out for the laptop pose, the map goes on the raised half, the screen, and the events and every control on the flat half, where fingers rest.
  • Folding redraws nothingThe layout keeps the crease clear even lying flat, so folding moves no cell. Fold, unfold or turn the simulated phone, and the tab, the selection and the open evidence stay put.
  • Clear of the systemThe layout puts no control on the crease, under the clock or the camera, or outside the safe area, and makes every target a finger tall. The tests check every tab on every device.

Try it in your terminal

$ go install github.com/WhitenWhiten/topophany/cmd/topo@main $ topo preview --device duo-laptop

[ and ] fold it through the poses, \ picks the next device, and the mouse stands in for a finger. The terminal needs about 99×70 cells. Until the next release, --device is on main.
